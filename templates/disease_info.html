Eye Disease Information

Learn about common eye conditions, associated risk factors, and general steps that support eye health.

Cataract

A cataract is clouding of the eye's natural lens. It can cause blurry vision, glare, faded colours, and difficulty seeing at night.

Causes and risk factors

Ageing is a major cause. Other risk factors include diabetes, smoking, eye injuries, prolonged corticosteroid use, and excessive ultraviolet exposure.

General guidance

Arrange an eye examination if vision changes. Sunglasses that block UV rays, not smoking, and managing existing health conditions can support eye health. Treatment decisions belong to an eye-care professional.

Diabetic Retinopathy

This condition affects retinal blood vessels and is associated with diabetes. It may progress without obvious early symptoms.

Causes and risk factors

Diabetes-related damage to retinal blood vessels is the underlying cause. Risk increases with longer duration of diabetes and can be influenced by blood glucose, blood pressure, and cholesterol control.

General guidance

People with diabetes should follow their clinician's recommended schedule for comprehensive dilated eye examinations and manage blood glucose, blood pressure, and cholesterol with their care team.

Glaucoma

Glaucoma refers to a group of eye conditions that damage the optic nerve. Some forms develop gradually and may cause no early symptoms.

Causes and risk factors

Risk factors include elevated eye pressure, increasing age, family history, and certain medical or eye conditions. Glaucoma can also occur when eye pressure is not elevated.

General guidance

Regular comprehensive eye examinations can help detect glaucoma. An eye-care professional can assess individual risk and recommend monitoring or treatment when needed. Sudden severe eye pain, a red eye, blurred vision, or nausea requires emergency assessment.

Normal Category

"Normal" is a label used by this project's dataset for images assigned to its normal class. It does not prove that an eye is healthy.

General guidance

Continue routine eye examinations based on your age, health, and clinician's advice. Seek professional assessment for new, persistent, or concerning vision changes.
